What can you pull?

One number in, and every Jayco your vehicle can tow — measured loaded, the way a scale would measure it.

Your vehicle's tow rating is in its owner's manual, on the driver's door jamb, or on the manufacturer's website under the exact trim and axle ratio you bought. Put it in below and we will show you every Jayco towable that comes in under it — compared against each trailer's gross vehicle weight rating, which is what it is allowed to weigh once you have loaded it, rather than the dry weight it left the factory at.

Before you tow anything

This tool compares one number you give us against each trailer's published gross vehicle weight rating. It is a shortlist, not a clearance to tow.

It does not account for your vehicle's tongue or pin weight limit, its gross combined weight rating, payload left after passengers and cargo, hitch class, brake controller, axle ratio, altitude, or the weight of anything already in the truck. Any one of those can rule out a trailer this page shows you.

Fifth wheels and toy haulers need a pickup with a bed and a fifth-wheel or gooseneck hitch. A tow rating alone does not make a vehicle suitable for one.

Weights are Jayco's published figures for the model year shown and are subject to change; options add weight. Verify against the trailer's own weight label and your vehicle's manual, and have a dealer confirm the match before purchase.

Because you never tow a dry trailer. Dry weight is what it left the factory weighing — no water, no propane, no batteries, no gear, no food. Gross vehicle weight rating is the most it is allowed to weigh with all of that in it. Matching on the dry figure at a 7,500 lb rating would show you 73 floorplans instead of 51, and twenty-two of those go over your limit the first time you fill the fresh tank.
