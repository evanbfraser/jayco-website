Tow vs. Drive Guide

Pull it, or drive it.

It is the first thing to settle and it decides most of what follows — what you spend, what you can choose from, and what your day looks like once you have parked.

A towable is a home with no engine in it: you supply the vehicle, and the two come apart when you arrive. A motorhome is one thing that drives — the living space and the drivetrain are the same purchase, and they stay together. Neither is the better answer. They are answers to different questions, and the figures below are the honest shape of the difference.

When towing is
the right call.

You already own the hard part, or you would rather your money went into the living space than into a second drivetrain.

  • The money goes into the coach

    A trailer has no engine, no chassis and no cab in its price, which is most of the gap in the table above. The same budget buys considerably more living space on a towable than it does on anything that drives itself.

  • There is far more to choose from

    Jayco has nearly three times as many towable floorplans as motorized ones, from a sixteen-foot single-axle to a forty-seven-foot fifth wheel. If a particular layout matters to you, the odds are better here.

  • You keep a car at camp

    Unhitch and the tow vehicle is yours for the week — the shop, the trailhead, the day you drive somewhere else entirely. Nothing gets packed away to go and buy milk.

Motorized

When driving is
the right call.

You would rather arrive, level it and stop — and you have no interest in owning a truck to make the rest of it work.

  • Nothing to hitch

    No coupling, no sway control, no reversing a trailer into a site in the dark. You park it, level it and you are set up — which is a real difference on a trip with a stop every night.

  • Everyone travels together

    Jayco fits a stress-tested belt at every designated seating position in its motorhomes, not only the two up front — so the people who are not driving are in the same space as the kettle and the bathroom.

  • No tow vehicle to match

    You are not shopping for a truck that suits the trailer, or measuring what the one on your drive can already pull. If you want the smallest step in, Jayco puts it at the Class C — easier to place and to park than a Class A.
