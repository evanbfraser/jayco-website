Sustainability & Overlander Packages

No hookup. No generator. No hurry.

Every Jayco towable leaves the line either solar-prepped or with a complete system already fitted. This is what that buys you.

The good sites are the ones with nothing at them. No power pedestal, no water spigot, no neighbour thirty feet away — and, for most people, no way to stay more than a night or two. Solar is what turns that into a week.

Jayco calls its solar programme Overlander, and it is not an aftermarket bracket on the roof. The panels, the controller and the wiring are specified with the coach, fitted on the line, and covered by the same warranty as everything else on it.

How it works

The roof charges the batteries.

That is the whole idea. Panels on the roof collect what the sun gives them, a controller regulates it, and the batteries hold it until you want it. Nothing is powered directly by the sun — everything is powered by the batteries, and the panels are what keeps putting charge back.

Which is why the battery matters as much as the panel. Jayco recommends lithium: it lasts longer, tolerates being run down further, and weighs less for the same usable capacity.

What is in a system

Four parts,
and one of them is safety.

  • The panels

    Rated in watts, which is a ceiling rather than a promise — 200W is what a panel makes in full sun, square to it, clean. Shade, angle and dust all take their cut, which is the honest reason a second panel is worth more than it looks on paper.

  • The solar controller

    Sits between the panels and the batteries and decides how the charge goes in. PWM is the simpler kind; MPPT is the one that finds the best working point on the panel and keeps it there, which is worth real percentage points on a cold bright day. RV-C means it talks to the rest of the coach rather than sitting on its own.

  • The inverter

    Turns the batteries' DC into the AC a household plug expects. Without one you have lights, pumps and USB; with one you have the outlets.

  • The DC-to-DC charger

    The part nobody puts on the brochure. It lets the tow vehicle charge the house batteries safely while you drive, at a rate the batteries will accept — which is what stops a long alternator run from cooking a lithium pack.

Overlander packages

Two specifications,
published in full.

One panel or two, and the difference an inverter makes. Both are 2026 model year, and on several product lines the package is standard rather than an option.

Overlander 1

Enough to hold the lights, the pump, the fridge control and the phones indefinitely. A weekend off-grid without thinking about it.

  • Solar 200W panel
  • Controller 30A Digital PWM
    30A MPPT RV-C on select models
  • Inverter Not included

Standard on 2026 Jay Feather products, 2026 Eagle HT travel trailers and Eagle fifth wheels.

Optional on 2026 Jay Flight SLX products and Eagle HT fifth wheels.

Overlander 2

Twice the panel and an 1800W inverter, which is the step that turns the household outlets on. A coffee machine, a laptop, a television.

  • Solar Two 200W panels
  • Controller 30A MPPT RV-C
  • Inverter 1800W RV-C

Standard on 2026 North Point and Pinnacle fifth wheels.

Optional on 2026 Jay Feather products, Eagle HT travel trailers and Eagle fifth wheels. On Jay Feather the package carries a Digital PWM controller and a non-RV-C inverter.

The Overlander range goes beyond these two. Jayco publishes a full specification for Overlander 1 and Overlander 2 on jayco.com/solar and does not publish the contents of the packages above them, so this page does not state them either — a guessed wattage is worse than a gap. A dealer can tell you what is fitted to a particular coach, and Build & Price shows what a given floorplan can be ordered with.
